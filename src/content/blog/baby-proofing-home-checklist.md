The moment your baby starts moving, your home transforms from a cozy nest into an obstacle course of hidden hazards. Outlets, cords, cabinets, stairs, that wobbly bookshelf — things you never noticed suddenly become risks at your baby's eye level. Baby-proofing isn't about bubble-wrapping your house; it's about removing the serious dangers so you can relax and let your little explorer explore. This room-by-room checklist covers when to start and exactly what to secure, prioritizing the hazards that matter most.

> 📌 **Key Takeaway:** According to the CDC, unintentional injuries in the home — falls, poisoning, choking, drowning, and burns — are a leading cause of injury in young children, and most are preventable. Baby-proof before your baby is mobile (around 6 months), and prioritize the big risks: furniture tip-overs, water, medicines, and small objects.

## When to Start Baby-Proofing

Start **before** your baby is mobile — ideally around **6 months**, before they're [crawling](/blog/baby-crawling-stages/) and pulling up. Babies move faster than parents expect, and it's easier to prepare in advance than to scramble after the first scary moment.

A great trick: **get down on the floor** and crawl through each room at your baby's level. You'll spot dangers you'd never notice standing up — the dangling cord, the sharp corner, the tempting outlet.

> ⚠️ **Furniture anchoring is non-negotiable:** A child is injured by furniture or TV tip-overs regularly, and these can be fatal. Every dresser, bookshelf, and TV must be anchored to the wall with anti-tip straps or brackets. Babies pull up and climb — assume they will.

## Room-by-Room Checklist

### Living Room
- ✅ Anchor bookshelves, TV stands, and TVs to the wall
- ✅ Cover electrical outlets
- ✅ Secure or shorten blind and curtain cords (strangulation risk) — use cordless where possible
- ✅ Add corner guards to sharp furniture edges (coffee tables)
- ✅ Remove or secure small decor, coins, and batteries

### Kitchen
- ✅ Install cabinet and drawer locks — especially where cleaners and knives live
- ✅ Use stove knob covers and turn pot handles inward when cooking
- ✅ Keep hot drinks and appliance cords out of reach
- ✅ Move cleaning products to a high, locked cabinet

### Bathroom
- ✅ **Never leave baby alone near water** — babies can drown in an inch
- ✅ Use a toilet lock
- ✅ Store medicines and toiletries locked and up high
- ✅ Set your water heater to 120°F (49°C) or lower to prevent scald burns
- ✅ Add a non-slip bath mat

### Nursery & Bedrooms
- ✅ Follow safe-sleep rules — bare crib, baby on back, no loose bedding
- ✅ Anchor the dresser and changing table
- ✅ Keep the crib away from windows, cords, and shelves
- ✅ Cover outlets and secure cords

### Stairs & Hallways
- ✅ Install safety gates at the top **and** bottom of stairs (hardware-mounted at the top)
- ✅ Ensure banister railings are close enough together that a head can't fit through
- ✅ Keep pathways clear

### Everywhere
- ✅ Outlet covers throughout
- ✅ Door-knob covers or locks on rooms with hazards
- ✅ Secure loose cords (electrical, charger, blind)
- ✅ Check floors constantly for small choking hazards
- ✅ Post the Poison Control number: **1-800-222-1222** (US)

## Don't Forget the "Invisible" Dangers

A few hazards parents commonly overlook:

- **Button batteries** — in remotes, toys, and cards; swallowing one can be fatal within hours
- **Magnets** — small high-powered magnets are extremely dangerous if swallowed
- **Laundry pods** — colorful and toxic; keep locked up
- **Houseplants** — some are poisonous
- **Purses and bags** — visitors' bags often hold medicines and small items

## Baby-Proofing Is Ongoing

Baby-proofing isn't one-and-done. As your baby grows into [walking](/blog/baby-walking-timeline/) and climbing, new dangers appear — they can reach higher, open more, and climb onto furniture. Re-crawl your home every few months, and stay a step ahead of each new skill. And remember: no amount of baby-proofing replaces supervision. Gadgets buy you seconds, not permission to look away.

## Common Questions About Baby-Proofing

### When should I start baby-proofing?

Start around 6 months, before your baby is crawling and pulling up. Babies become mobile faster than parents expect, so it's better to prepare early than to react after a scary moment. Anchoring furniture, covering outlets, and securing hazards ahead of time lets you relax as your baby starts to explore.

### What is the most important baby-proofing step?

Anchoring furniture and TVs to the wall is arguably the most critical, because tip-overs can be fatal and babies inevitably pull up and climb. Alongside that, securing water areas, locking away medicines and chemicals, and removing choking hazards address the risks most likely to cause serious harm.

### Do I really need safety gates?

If you have stairs, yes — safety gates at the top and bottom prevent dangerous falls, which are a leading home injury for babies. Use a hardware-mounted gate at the top of stairs for maximum security. Gates also help section off rooms with hazards, giving your baby a safe zone to explore.

### Are outlet covers enough for electrical safety?

Outlet covers are a good start, but complete electrical safety also means securing dangling cords (a strangulation and pull-down risk), keeping appliances and chargers out of reach, and covering power strips. Sliding outlet covers that self-close are more effective than small plug-in caps, which can become choking hazards themselves.

### How do I baby-proof against button batteries and magnets?

Keep all button batteries and small magnets completely out of reach and secured — swallowing either can cause severe, rapid internal injury. Check that battery compartments in remotes, toys, and gadgets are screwed shut. If you suspect your child swallowed a battery or magnet, seek emergency care immediately; it's a true emergency.

## The Bottom Line

Baby-proofing lets your baby explore safely and lets you breathe. Keep these principles in mind:

- **Start around 6 months**, before your baby is mobile
- **Prioritize the big four** — tip-overs, water, poisons, and choking hazards
- **Anchor all furniture and TVs** — this is non-negotiable
- **Watch the invisible dangers** — button batteries, magnets, laundry pods
- **It's ongoing** — re-proof as your baby grows, and never rely on gadgets over supervision

Crawl through your home at baby level this weekend and tackle the big risks first. A few hours of preparation buys you months of peace of mind as your little one discovers the world.
