A surprising number of the most traditional English names are German in origin. William, Henry, Charles, Robert, Richard, Emma, and Alice all came into English from Germanic languages, most of them with the Normans in 1066.

Old Germanic names were built like compound words: two elements joined together, each with a meaning. *Wil* (will) plus *helm* (helmet, protection) gives Wilhelm, or William. Once you know a handful of these elements, you can read the meaning of dozens of names. This list has 60, with the meaning of each and the German pronunciation where it differs from English.

> 📌 **Key Takeaway:** Most classic German names are two-part compounds, and the same building blocks repeat: *bert* means bright, *rich* means ruler, *hard* means brave or hardy, *wolf* means wolf, and *fried* means peace. That is why German names so often mean things like "bright fame" (Robert) or "peaceful ruler" (Frederick). ## What German Parents Choose Today

The names above are the German classics, but they are not what you would hear on a German playground today. Current favorites in Germany are short and international: **Emilia, Emma, Mia, Sophia, Hannah, and Lina** for girls, and **Noah, Matteo, Elias, Leon, Paul, and Luca** for boys. Most of those are not Germanic in origin at all.

At the same time, some grandparent names are returning, just as they are in English. **Frieda, Ida, Greta, Mathilda, and Lotte** are in fashion again for girls, along with **Oskar, Emil, Anton, Theo, and Karl** for boys.

So you have two honest options. If you want a name that sounds German to English ears, choose from the classics. If you want a name a child in Berlin might actually have today, choose from the revival list.

## Pronunciation Tips

**W is pronounced like V.** Wolfgang is VOLF-gahng and Wilhelm is VIL-helm.

**A final -e is pronounced.** Lotte is LOT-teh, not "lot." Anneliese ends in -zeh.

**S before a vowel sounds like Z.** Siegfried begins with a Z sound, and Liesel is LEE-zel.

**The -ich and -ig endings are soft.** In Dietrich, Ulrich, and Ludwig, the ending is a soft, breathy sound that English does not have. English speakers usually replace it with a K, which is widely accepted.

If your family will use the English pronunciation, that is perfectly fine. Just decide early, so that grandparents, teachers, and the child all use the same one.

## FAQ

### What are strong German boy names?

Conrad (bold counsel), Leopold (bold people), Bernard (brave as a bear), Richard (brave ruler), and Walter (ruler of the army) all carry meanings of strength or leadership. Otto and Hugo are shorter options with the same classic feel.

### What are pretty German girl names?

Greta, Frieda, Matilda, Liesel, Lotte, and Romy are widely liked. Adelaide and Wilhelmina are longer, more formal choices with the short forms Heidi and Mina.

### What are the most popular baby names in Germany?

Recent favorites in Germany include Emilia, Emma, Mia, Sophia, and Hannah for girls, and Noah, Matteo, Elias, Leon, and Paul for boys. Rankings change each year and vary between regions.

### Why do so many English names come from German?

English is a Germanic language, and many Germanic names were also brought to England by the Normans after 1066. William, Henry, Robert, Richard, Emma, and Alice all arrived that way.
